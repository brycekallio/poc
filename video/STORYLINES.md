# P.O.C demo film: three storylines

**The one thing:** P.O.C finds what's unclear in your own draft, and asks before it rewrites.
**The viewer:** someone typing a vague request, in the box where they already work.
**The real data that travels through the film:** the draft and questions from the extension's own
preview page (`code/extension/preview/demo.html`). They are sample content, shown in the real interface:

> write a summary of the q3 report for the team, make it good and not too long but cover everything. use the numbers from the finance section

**Assumptions for all three:** 20-30 s, 1920x1080 at 30 fps, captions on screen, no voice. Sound is free
bundled effects on visible actions only, with no music bed: HeyGen isn't installed and there's no local
music model. Interface comes from P.O.C itself, running in a browser.

---

## A. Not Too Long But Cover Everything (recommended)

A stranger sees their own bad habit in the first second, and the rewrite happens to those same words.

- **Device and form:** the feature as the hook, in one frame that rebuilds.
- **What you see:** the real draft fills the screen with one self-contradicting phrase picked out. The
  dot appears, the phrase lifts out of the draft and becomes P.O.C's question, and the answer rebuilds
  the draft in place into a clear prompt.
- **First two seconds:** frame 0 is the draft at giant size, already on screen. "not too long but
  cover everything" turns solid black while the rest greys out. Caption: "You can't have both."
- **Beats:** contradiction → dot arrives at the caret → hover and click Coach this draft → phrase
  becomes the question → click an answer → second question, quick → draft rebuilds in place → used in
  the box → p●c end card with the install link.
- **Look:** white ground, near-black ink, Geist, P.O.C's own panel at 1.6-2x. The glass dot is the only
  shiny thing in the film.
- **Sound:** soft key ticks as the draft rebuilds, one real click per answer, a pop on each question,
  one riser into the end mark.

## B. Same Draft, Twice

The cleanest argument for why asking first matters, but the first half shows AI output we wrote ourselves.

- **Device and form:** life before and after, split screen collapsing to one.
- **What you see:** the same draft is sent twice side by side. The left gets a long generic reply and
  keeps scrolling while the right stops to ask one question, then the left is dropped and the right
  fills the frame with the clear prompt.
- **First two seconds:** two identical draft boxes and two cursors hit send on the same frame.
- **Beats:** same draft twice → left floods, right asks → answer → left falls away → prompt lands →
  claim → end card.
- **Look:** the same white and ink, with the left pane in grey. The left reply carries a visible
  "Example" label, since it isn't real AI output.
- **Sound:** a nearly silent left side with one click and pop on the right, then a riser on the prompt.

## C. The Dot Gets Around (the bold one)

This sells "every text box", which is the broadest pitch, but it spends less time on what makes the questions good.

- **Device and form:** a character does the work, in one continuous take.
- **What you see:** the dot is the hero. It rides the caret through an AI prompt, an email and a chat
  message on one long unbroken surface, and adapts each time: prompt coaching for the AI, message
  coaching for people.
- **First two seconds:** extreme close-up of the glass dot, then the camera pulls back to reveal it's
  sitting at the end of an unfinished sentence.
- **Beats:** dot close-up → pull back → coaches a prompt → slides to an email → coaches for a person →
  slides to a chat → off switch flips it dark → flips on → end card.
- **Look:** three plain text boxes with no real brands, on one long white surface. The dot is 4-5x its
  real size.
- **Sound:** a small tick each time the dot lands, one whoosh per move, and the light-switch click as
  the punchline.
